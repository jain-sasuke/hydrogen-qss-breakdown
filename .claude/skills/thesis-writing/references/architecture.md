# Structure

## The backbone

The reader should be able to reconstruct this chain after finishing the thesis,
whether or not it maps onto chapter boundaries:

context → problem → gap → question → approach → verification → discovery →
explanation → limits → contribution

Concretely, the thesis answers: what problem exists; why it matters; what is
currently assumed; when that assumption might fail; what precise question this
work asks; what was required to answer it; why the method suits; why the
implementation can be trusted; what the model reveals; what causes it; how large
it is; when it matters; what it changes about the original problem; what remains
open.

A chapter that cannot be attached to this chain needs a reason to be in the main
thesis rather than an appendix.

## Chapters

Fix four things before drafting:

- **Entry state** — what the reader knows from earlier chapters.
- **Chapter question** — the intellectual problem now to be solved.
- **Contribution** — what they will know at the end.
- **Exit bridge** — why the next chapter becomes necessary.

**Opening.** Start from the unresolved problem inherited from the previous
chapter, not from generic field background supplied because chapters normally
have introductions. *The preceding analysis identifies two competing timescales,
but timescale separation alone does not determine whether a spectroscopic
inversion fails; the relevant quantity is the error in the observable itself.*
That is continuity. A paragraph restating what plasma spectroscopy is, is not.

**Closing.** End when the intellectual task is done. If a bridge is needed, the
last sentence should raise the next question rather than summarise the section
the reader has just read. Appending a summary to every subsection trains the
reader to skip endings.

## Sections

Use whichever shape the science takes:

- motivation → construction → consequence
- question → evidence → interpretation → consequence
- physical question → governing principle → assumptions → derivation → checks →
  meaning

Do not impose one shape mechanically; identical structure across every section
is one of the clearest tells of generated text.

## Story, not suspense

Scientific storytelling is not withholding. Give the destination early enough
that the reader understands why the journey matters: question → reasoning →
evidence → interpretation, rather than accumulated information ending in a
reveal.

## Paragraphs

One intellectual unit each. Where useful: connect to what is established, develop
one claim, end on the consequence or the unresolved point that deserves
emphasis. Do not manufacture four-sentence paragraphs — uniform paragraph length
is another generated-text tell.

## Sentences

Move from known to new where it helps continuity. Keep subject near verb. Put
the action in the verb rather than burying it in an abstract noun: *we tested how
the ratio changes with density*, not *an investigation of the sensitivity of the
ratio was performed*. Use the passive when the process genuinely deserves the
focus; banning it mechanically produces its own awkwardness.

## Transitions must carry logic

A transition establishes why B follows A. *Furthermore, electron collisions are
important* asserts a connection without making one. *Radiative decay alone cannot
repopulate the neighbouring angular-momentum states; at divertor densities,
electron and proton collisions provide the competing redistribution pathway*
makes the connection the reason for the sentence.

Prefer intellectual transitions to navigation announcements. Use an explicit
roadmap only where the structure is complicated enough that it genuinely saves
the reader effort.

## The repetition ledger

Track, for every substantive fact, whether it has been introduced, defined,
derived, demonstrated, interpreted, or concluded. A fact may recur when its
function changes:

- Introduction: the QSS approximation eliminates the excited-state time
  derivatives — *defining it*.
- Methods: the resulting algebraic system — *constructing it*.
- Results: the transient deviation measures the consequence of eliminating those
  derivatives — *interpreting it*.

That is progression. Stating the same definition in all three places is not.

## Progressive compression

Once introduced properly, refer to a concept more compactly: full explanation,
then a short reminder if needed, then the technical term or symbol alone. The
thesis should get denser as the reader gets better, which is also what makes a
long thesis readable.

## Terminology

One term per concept. If "excited-state relaxation time", "fast relaxation time"
and "atomic equilibration time" mean different things, define the distinction; if
they mean the same thing, pick one. Repeating a technical noun is preferable to
synonym rotation that makes the reader wonder whether something changed.

## Detail allocation

Spend words in proportion to difficulty and importance. Expand original
derivations, unfamiliar assumptions, unusual methodology, counterintuitive
results, central mechanisms, and the places an examiner will push. Compress
standard algebra, textbook definitions, routine software operations, repeated
methodology, and anything already established.

No section should exist to demonstrate that the author knows something.
