\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

A Balmer line-ratio measurement in a tokamak divertor is inverted for electron
temperature, at a density supplied by an independent measurement, against a
table computed from a collisional--radiative model at fixed $(\Te,\nel)$. Indexing that table on two axes requires the
neutral-to-ion ratio to be a function of those two axes alone, which holds only
once the ionisation balance has settled. A divertor during an edge-localised
mode has not settled. Careful practice avoids the difficulty rather than
assuming it away, by choosing high Balmer lines in detached conditions where one
supply channel dominates and the neutral-to-ion ratio cancels; that escape is
not available for $H_\alpha$ and $H_\beta$, where neither channel dominates.
\textbf{What follows is the cost of that closure where it is adopted, not a
demonstration that divertor spectroscopy fails.}

This thesis measures what that closure costs. A $43$-state time-dependent
collisional--radiative model of atomic hydrogen, resolved in $\ell$ to $n=8$ and
bundled one state per shell to $n=15$, is built over $50$ temperatures from $1$
to \SI{10}{\electronvolt} and $8$ densities from $10^{12}$ to
\SI{e15}{\per\cubic\centi\metre} and integrated through abrupt changes in
temperature.

The approximation usually held responsible is not the one that fails. Measured
along the full $43$-state trajectory, the quasi-steady-state closure for the
excited manifold is accurate to $8.66\times10^{-6}$ at the reference point
$\Te = \benchTe$, $\nel = \benchne$, and to $6.73\times10^{-9}$ at the grid
point carrying the largest diagnostic error. What does not keep up is the ground
state, which holds almost every atom and turns over on a clock four orders of
magnitude slower than the excited levels.

The cost of assuming it has turned over factorises exactly. With $u$ the
ground-to-ion ratio, the error in the $n=3$ to $n=4$ population ratio on the
plateau between the two clocks is
$\epsplat = |\exp(\overline{S}\,G\,\Delta\ln\Te)-1|$, where $\overline{S}$ is
the sensitivity of the line ratio to the reservoir and
$G = \Delta\ln u/\Delta\ln\Te$ is very nearly a property of the operating
point rather than of the disturbance. Across a fourfold change in step size $G$
moves by at most \SI{6.74}{\percent}, a drift that is itself the secant closing
on its tangent, while $\epsplat$ moves by a factor $8.44$; so the
two coefficients rather than a percentage are the reportable object. The
sensitivity obeys a ceiling, $|f_3-f_4| < 1$, that no atomic dataset can
exceed, because it follows from the structure of the linear system and not
from any rate coefficient; for a given dataset the sharper form is
$\max|f_3-f_4| = \tanh(|\Delta|/4)$, and $\Delta$ is atomic data.

Averaged over an event of the duration extrapolated to the ITER pedestal,
\SI{506}{\micro\second}, $24$ of the $448$ point-and-direction pairs above
\SI{2}{\electronvolt} carry an error above \SI{10}{\percent}, the worst
\SI{15.3}{\percent}; over the \SI{100}{\micro\second} the maps use it is $45$
and \SI{17.5}{\percent}. Counted on the exposure-integrated line ratio a
detector records, rather than on the time-averaged error, the
\SI{100}{\micro\second} census is $43$ of $448$. The count is sensitive to the event duration and the
worst case is not, falling by \SI{18}{\percent} across a factor $7.5$ in it.
Those are fixed-density figures. Stepping density up with temperature, as a
two-parameter stress test rather than a model of an edge-localised mode, leaves
the count near a tenth of the pairs
available and raises the worst case to \SI{22.1}{\percent}, so for that perturbation the
fixed-density map understates it. At citable detached-divertor densities none
of the $108$ pairs exceeds \SI{10}{\percent} at either duration, and the worst
case is \SI{7.2}{\percent}. Inverted for temperature at known
density, the plateau ratio has no solution within the table's $1$ to
\SI{10}{\electronvolt} domain for $166$ of those $448$ pairs, and where it has
one, a \SI{4.81}{\percent} step is read back with a median error of
\SI{48}{\percent}, on the side the plasma came from at every pair. Timescale
separation licenses the reduction and predicts none of this: the ratio of the two clocks is $9982$ at
the reference point, and within the defended range the density at which that
ratio is largest sits a factor $19.3$ away from the density at which the error
is largest, which is \SI{18.1}{\percent} against \SI{7.4}{\percent} there.

Two boundaries on the result are measured rather than assumed. Below
\SI{2}{\electronvolt}, holding the electron density fixed contradicts
conservation of nuclei and the Lyman-$\alpha$ optical depth passes unity, and
the two boundaries fall in the same narrow band. Above it, the closed-parcel
assumption on which every magnitude depends fails at all $45$ breakdown pairs,
because a long ionisation time is exactly the condition under which neutral
transport rather than local ionisation sets the ground-state density. The
structural results survive that. The magnitudes are conditional on it, and are
reported as such.
