\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

A Balmer line ratio is inverted for divertor electron temperature at known
density against a collisional--radiative table at fixed $(\Te,\nel)$. Indexing
on two axes requires the neutral-to-ion ratio to be a function of those axes
alone, true only after ionisation balance settles, which a divertor during an
edge-localised mode has not. That escape, one channel dominating so the ratio
cancels, is unavailable for $H_\alpha$ and $H_\beta$. \textbf{What follows is
the cost of that closure where it is adopted, not a demonstration that divertor
spectroscopy fails.}

A $43$-state time-dependent model of atomic hydrogen, resolved in $\ell$ to
$n=8$ and bundled to $n=15$, is integrated through abrupt temperature steps on
a $50$ by $8$ grid over $1$ to \SI{10}{\electronvolt} and $10^{12}$ to
\SI{e15}{\per\cubic\centi\metre}.

The approximation usually blamed is not the one that fails: the
quasi-steady-state closure for the excited manifold holds to
$8.66\times10^{-6}$ at the reference point. What lags is the ground state,
holding almost every atom and turning over four orders of magnitude slower.

That cost factorises exactly as
$\epsplat = |\exp(\overline{S}\,G\,\Delta\ln\Te)-1|$, with $\overline{S}$ the
reservoir sensitivity of the line ratio and $G$ the reservoir gain. The
sensitivity obeys a ceiling, $|f_3-f_4| < 1$, set by the structure of the
linear system, not by any rate coefficient.

Over \SI{100}{\micro\second}, $45$ of the $448$ pairs above
\SI{2}{\electronvolt} exceed \SI{10}{\percent} error, the worst
\SI{17.5}{\percent}; timescale separation licenses the reduction and predicts
none of it. Inverted, the
ratio has no solution for $166$, reaches one only across a fold for $44$, and
over the remaining $238$ reads a \SI{4.81}{\percent} step back with
\SI{48}{\percent} median error.

Two measured boundaries exclude the model below \SI{2}{\electronvolt}. Above
it, the closed-parcel assumption every magnitude depends on fails at all $45$
breakdown pairs. The structural results survive that; the magnitudes are conditional on it.
