The Cost of Assuming Ionisation Balance

A divertor's temperature is read from the ratio of two hydrogen lines, Hα at 656 nm and Hβ at 486 nm, looked up in a table. This page walks through what that table silently assumes, which assumption actually fails after a disturbance, and how large the resulting error is across the operating range.

The measurement

A table with two axes, and a ratio with three arguments

An atom in the n = 3 level got there by one of two routes. Either it was a ground-state neutral kicked up by an electron collision, fed from below, or it was an ion that captured an electron and cascaded down, fed from above. Because the rate equations are linear, every excited population splits exactly into those two supplies:

np = ap n1s + cp nion

The coefficients ap and cp depend only on electron temperature Te and density ne. Put that into the observed ratio and divide by the ion density:

Robs ∝ a3 u + c3a4 u + c4,   u = n1s/nion

The neutral-to-ion ratio u is a third argument. It is set by the plasma's recent history and by transport, not by the local Te and ne. A table indexed on two axes has to dispose of it somehow.

Route one · used in practice

Work where one supply dominates both levels, so u cancels. This holds for n ≥ 5 in detached plasmas (Lomanowski et al. 2015). It is unavailable for n = 3 and 4, where neither supply dominates.

Route two · what this thesis prices

Close it: assume ionisation balance (collisional–radiative equilibrium, CRE) and take the u that CRE predicts for the local conditions. That asserts the plasma has finished changing. A divertor during an edge-localised mode (ELM) has not.

This is not a demonstration that divertor spectroscopy fails. It is a measurement of what the equilibrium-reservoir closure costs where it is adopted.

The model

Two clocks, four orders of magnitude apart

The model is a 43-state time-dependent description of atomic hydrogen, resolved in orbital angular momentum ℓ up to n = 8 and bundled to n = 15, integrated through abrupt temperature steps on a 50 × 8 grid spanning 1–10 eV and 1012–1015 cm−3. The rate matrix has two natural timescales: the 42 excited levels relax on τrelax, while the ground state, which holds almost every atom, turns over on τQSS.

The finding

The closure everyone worries about is not the one that fails

A two-axis table needs two approximations at once, and they are routinely conflated. Integrating the full system through the step and scoring both along the same trajectory separates them.

Holds

Quasi-steady state of the excited levels

The Bodenstein approximation applied to atomic levels: the excited manifold keeps up with whatever reservoir is present.

εQSS = 8.66×10−6

at the benchmark; 6.73×10−9 at [0,4], the point with the largest diagnostic error.

Fails

Ionisation balance of the reservoir

That n1s/nion stands at its CRE value for the local conditions. After a step it stays wrong for ~τQSS.

εCRE = 6.34×10−2

at the benchmark; 0.387 at [0,4].

The excited levels re-equilibrate in nanoseconds onto a reservoir that has not moved, and report that partial-equilibrium state faithfully. The ratio is measured correctly; the table then assigns it to the wrong plasma.

Why the error is bounded

Two logistic curves, and a ceiling no dataset can raise

Call fm the fraction of shell m that is fed from below. In the variable x = ln u it is exactly a logistic of unit width,

fm(x) = 1 / (1 + e−(x − xm)),   xm = ln(cm/am)

The ratio's sensitivity to the reservoir is d ln R/d ln u = f3 − f4. Both curves have the same shape and differ only by the shift Δ = x4 − x3, so their difference can never exceed tanh(|Δ|/4) < 1.

The unit width comes from the populations being affine in n1s, a property of the linear system, not of any rate coefficient. Consequence: a reservoir that is stale by a factor of two cannot move the line ratio by more than a factor of two, for any shell pair, any conditions, any atomic dataset. The height of the ceiling does depend on the data, through Δ.

What a diagnostician can use

The error factorises exactly

εplat = | exp( S̄ · G · ΔlnTe ) − 1 |

S̄ is the mean sensitivity of the ratio to the reservoir, the average of f3 − f4 over the interval the reservoir traverses. G = Δlnu/ΔlnTe is the reservoir gain, the secant of the CRE curve. Both are negative everywhere, so their product is positive.

The step size is the event's; S̄ and G belong to the operating point. A percentage quoted without its step is not a property of the plasma: across 736 point-and-direction triples, |G| moves by at most 6.74 % between step sizes of one, two and four grid intervals, while εplat changes by up to a factor 8.44.

A line ratio carries no record of the transient, so this is a forward criterion: the step has to be estimated from outside the spectrum.

Line: the formula with the one-interval S̄ and G held fixed (the small-step reading). Dots: the model's own εplat at steps of one, two and four grid intervals, where S̄ and G are re-evaluated for each step.

    Scope

    What this work cannot claim

    The structural results, the two-channel split, the unit-width logistic and the ceiling tanh(|Δ|/4), are properties of the rate matrix at fixed conditions. The magnitudes are conditional on more.

    Magnitudes assume a closed parcel

    They assume the ground state evolves by local atomic physics alone. Comparing neutral escape time with τQSS, none of the 45 breakdown pairs above 2 eV satisfies τesc > τQSS. The magnitudes are reference values, neither bounds nor estimates of the divertor error.

    Not a result below 2 eV

    Two measured boundaries exclude the cold edge: holding ne fixed stops being self-consistent (contour at 1.41–1.50 eV), and Lyman lines become optically thick (1.01–2.36 eV across slab thicknesses).

    No molecules

    A molecular supply feeding n = 3 preferentially would change f3 − f4. Imposed emissivity fractions reduce the plateau error at the crest by factors of 5.4 to 8.1. It is a sensitivity scenario, not a bound.

    Inverting the ratio is worse than the ratio

    Over the 448 defended pairs the perturbed ratio has no temperature solution for 166, reaches one only across a fold for 44, and for the remaining 238 reads a 4.81 % step back with 48 % median error.

    Atomic data: sensitivities, not a budget

    Swapping excitation for R-matrix values and ionisation for Lotz moves εplat by 15.5 % over the defended pairs (at most 25.4 %) and M by 11 %.

    The crest is not a detachment criterion

    The density crest sits a factor 5.2 below the detached band; at citable detached densities the 100 µs estimate never reaches 10 %.
