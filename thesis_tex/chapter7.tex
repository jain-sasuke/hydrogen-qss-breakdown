\chapter{What It Means}
\label{ch:conclusions}

The physicist of Chapter~\ref{ch:intro} is still at her console. Nothing in this
thesis takes her spectrometer away, alters the two wavelengths she measures, or
questions the ratio she forms from them. What it changes is the moment at which
she is entitled to look that ratio up. This chapter states that condition, hands
over the two numbers that convert it into an error estimate for her own
transient rather than for the one modelled here, and says how much of the
reasoning behind it belongs to this work and how much to a literature that is
sixty years old.

%=============================================================================
\section{The suspect was innocent}
\label{sec:what_found}
% QUESTION: what did the thesis set out to test, and what did it find instead?
%=============================================================================

Chapter~\ref{ch:intro} asked how large the error in a $(\Te,\nel)$ inversion of
the $H_\alpha/H_\beta$ ratio becomes when the plasma is disturbed faster than
its ionisation balance can relax, what physical quantity sets that error, and
where in the operating range it is largest. It also named a suspect. The
quasi-steady-state (QSS) approximation eliminates several dozen dynamical
variables by asserting that the excited levels equilibrate faster than
conditions change, and the expectation was that some of them would fail to keep
up.

They keep up. Integrating the full 43-state system through a temperature step
and evaluating both error measures along the same trajectory gives
$\epsQSS = 8.66\times10^{-6}$ at the benchmark point and
$\epsQSS = 6.73\times10^{-9}$ at $[0,4]$, the grid point carrying the largest
diagnostic error anywhere (Section~\ref{sec:qss_is_exact}). The closure is at
its most accurate exactly where the tabulated answer is at its worst. There is
no intermediate eigenvalue for an excited population to lag on: the gap between
the slow mode and the fast band is $3.7\times10^{7}$ at that point, so a level
that responds in nanoseconds cannot lag a reservoir that moves in microseconds.

What fails is the other assumption, the one Section~\ref{sec:the_assumption}
separated out and then left standing. A table with two axes has to dispose of a
third argument, the neutral-to-ion ratio $u = \ngs/\nion$, and the rule it uses
to dispose of it is equilibrium ionisation balance. That rule asserts that the
plasma has finished changing. For the whole of $\tauslow$ after a disturbance it
is false, and the excited manifold, having re-equilibrated in nanoseconds onto a
reservoir that has not moved, reports the partial-equilibrium state faithfully.
The ratio is measured correctly and the table then assigns it to the wrong
plasma.

If the fast states were the problem, the repair would be a time-dependent solve
of the excited manifold, and Section~\ref{sec:qss_is_exact} shows that such a
solve returns an answer already correct to eight decimal places. The repair
belongs instead at the closure applied to $u$, which is a statement about the
plasma's recent history and not about its atoms. Calling the failure a
breakdown of the quasi-steady-state approximation would point the effort at the
one part of the calculation that is working.

\section{What to do at the console}
\label{sec:criterion}
% QUESTION: given a measured ratio and a plasma that has recently been
% disturbed, what should a diagnostician compute before trusting the table?
%=============================================================================

The first thing to say is what the error is not. It is not a bias that can be
determined once and subtracted. Its size depends on the transient the plasma has
just undergone, and a single line ratio contains no record of that transient:
the same measured number is returned by an equilibrated plasma and by a
displaced one. No amount of care with the spectrometer separates them, and no
correction factor tabulated on $(\Te,\nel)$ can carry the information, because
the information is not a function of $(\Te,\nel)$.

What can be tabulated is the plasma's \emph{response} to being displaced, and
that is a property of the operating point alone. Section~\ref{sec:exact_decomposition}
factorises the plateau error exactly into a sensitivity and a displacement, and
Equation~\eqref{eq:gain_def} splits the displacement again into a gain and
the size of the transient, leaving
\begin{equation}
  \epsplat \;=\; \Big|\exp\!\big(\overline{S}\,G\,\Delta\ln\Te\big)-1\Big| ,
  \qquad
  \lim_{\Delta\ln\Te\to0}\frac{\epsplat}{\big|\Delta\ln\Te\big|}
  \;=\; \big|\overline{S}\,G\big| .
  \label{eq:recipe}
\end{equation}

\noindent This repeats Eq.~\eqref{eq:eps_of_step}, and it is repeated because
its function has changed. In Chapter~\ref{ch:results} it was the end of a
derivation; here it is a procedure, and the reader supplies the third factor. It
inherits that chapter's assumption that the ion population is a fixed reservoir
and that $\ngs$ is frozen for the duration of the event, which removes the
freedom for ionisation balance to respond and is the condition
Section~\ref{sec:transport} cannot test.

Both structural factors are dimensionless. The mean sensitivity $\overline{S}$
is the average of $\ffrac{3}-\ffrac{4}$ over the interval the reservoir
traverses, and it says how strongly the observed ratio answers to the
ground-state reservoir. The reservoir gain $G = \Delta\ln u/\Delta\ln\Te$,
the secant along the CRE curve over the step taken, says how far equilibrium
ionisation balance would move that reservoir for a
given fractional change in temperature. Both $\overline{S}$ and $G$ are
negative everywhere on the grid, in both step directions: heating depletes the
ground state and cooling refills it, so $\Delta\ln u$ and $\Delta\ln\Te$ always
carry opposite signs, and the ground-fed fraction of $n=3$ exceeds that of
$n=4$ at every point. Their product is positive for heating and negative for
cooling, and $\epsplat$ is positive in both because
Eq.~\eqref{eq:recipe} takes a modulus.

\paragraph{The procedure.} For a CR matrix of one's own, at one's own operating
point:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Solve the excited manifold twice, once with the ion reservoir alone and
once with the ground state alone, to obtain the population coefficients $a_p$
and $c_p$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:two_supplies_intro} for the two emitting shells.
These are the same two solves any steady-state CR table already performs. Form
the switching points $x_m = \ln(c_m/a_m)$ and their separation
$\Delta = x_4 - x_3$; the two ground-fed fractions are then the single logistic
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:f_logistic}, displaced by $\Delta$.
\item Differentiate the equilibrium reservoir with respect to temperature at
fixed density to get $G$.
\item Insert the transient one actually cares about as $\Delta\ln\Te$. The
reservoir excursion is $\Delta\ln u = G\,\Delta\ln\Te$, and that fixes the
interval over which $\overline{S}$ is the mean of $\ffrac{3}-\ffrac{4}$.
\end{enumerate}

Steps 1 and 2 belong to the operating point and are done once; step 3 belongs to
the event. The sensitivity straddles the two, because the curves are the
operating point's and the interval is the event's, and in the small-step limit
it collapses onto the operating point as well: $\overline{S}$ tends to
$\ffrac{3}-\ffrac{4}$ evaluated at the undisturbed reservoir, which is why
$|\overline{S}G|$ can be tabulated on $(\Te,\nel)$ alone. That division is the
whole reason for reporting structure instead of a percentage. Measured across
the grid, $|G|$ moves by at most \SI{6.74}{\percent}
between step sizes of one, two and four grid intervals, over the $736$
point-and-direction triples for which all three step sizes were computed, while
$\epsplat$ at the same points changes by up to a factor $8.44$. A percentage
quoted without its step is not a property of the plasma; $G$ very nearly is.
Both figures are direct reductions of
\texttt{validation/reservoir\_gain/reservoir\_gain.csv} ($2288$ rows, stamped
10 September 2026). Both aggregations are performed by
\texttt{verify\_reservoir\_gain.py} and written to
\texttt{validation/reservoir\_gain/reservoir\_gain\_summary.csv}, one row per
filter, because the same quantity over a different filter is a different number
and this is where the two used to be confused. The spread of $|G|$ is $1.039$ to
$1.065$ over $368$ heating triples and $1.039$ to $1.067$ over $368$ cooling
triples; the spread of $\epsplat$ over the same points is $2.27$ to $8.44$
heating and $2.33$ to $4.70$ cooling.

\paragraph{The scale, before anyone computes anything.} Over the $982$ heating
steps of that file that have a resolvable plateau window, $|\overline{S}|$ runs
from $0.0649$ to $0.4822$; over all $2288$ rows $|G|$ runs from $2.640$ to
$14.519$. Restricted to the range this thesis defends, $\Te\ge\SI{2}{\electronvolt}$,
those become $0.0649$ to $0.4552$ and $2.640$ to $7.822$, and the product
$|\overline{S}G|$ runs from $0.362$ to $3.535$. The upper end is the useful
number to remember. In the worst conditions defended here, a \SI{1}{\percent}
temperature excursion costs the Balmer inversion about \SI{3.5}{\percent} in the
line ratio; at the benchmark point it costs about \SI{1.3}{\percent}.

\subsection{Five things that follow, and one that does not}

\paragraph{The response has a ceiling, and it is below unity.} Because both
ground-fed fractions are the same unit-width logistic curve displaced by
$\Delta$, their difference cannot exceed $\tanh(|\Delta|/4)$, and $\overline{S}$
inherits that bound (Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sbar_bound}). The $\tanh$ comes from the
width of the logistic, and the width is unity because the excited populations
are exactly affine in $\ngs$, which is a property of the linear system rather
than of any rate coefficient. Every property of the 42-state network enters only
through $\Delta$. Since $|\mathrm{d}\ln\Robs/\mathrm{d}\ln\bdep| < 1$ everywhere,
integrating over any excursion gives $|\Delta\ln\Robs| \le |\Delta\ln\bdep|$, so a
reservoir left stale by a factor of two cannot put the line ratio out by more
than a factor of two. A stale reservoir is attenuated in logarithmic space,
never amplified. That protection holds for every shell pair, every
$(\Te,\nel)$ and every atomic dataset, because the width of the logistic is
unity whatever the rates are. The \emph{height} of the ceiling is not
dataset-independent in the same way: it is $\tanh(|\Delta|/4)$, and $\Delta$ is
built from rate coefficients. The ceiling is approached most closely where the
error is largest: at $[23,3]$, one column into the crest, the measured
sensitivity already sits at \SI{97}{\percent} of it
(Section~\ref{sec:bound}). That is a statement about the headroom beneath the
ceiling and not about where the ceiling sits, and the two must not be run
together. Substituting an independent excitation dataset moves the ceiling
while leaving the headroom nearly where it was, the median sensitivity falling
from \SI{80.4}{\percent} to \SI{78.2}{\percent} of its own cap over the $338$
heating pairs of the map (Sections~\ref{sec:atomic_propagation}
and~\ref{sec:bound}). Substituting the ionisation data instead does the
opposite, moving the sensitivity inside a cap that barely shifts. Running at
\SI{97}{\percent} of the cap is therefore not an artefact of this rate set, but
neither does it bound where a refinement would put the cap itself, and the two
refinements this thesis can perform move the two quantities separately.

\paragraph{The clock is $\tauslow$, and it is far too slow to average away.} The
temptation is to assume that an error introduced by a fast disturbance decays
with the fast timescale. It does not. The error decays as a single exponential
on $\tauslow$, with a half-life of \SI{12.95}{\micro\second} at the benchmark and
\SI{1.10}{\milli\second} at $[15,3]$ (Section~\ref{sec:persistence}), against
the \SI{2.28}{\nano\second} on which the excited manifold responds. A
\SI{100}{\micro\second} exposure does not integrate the error away.
Counted on the time-averaged estimate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:elm_bound}, which direct
43-state propagation places within \SI{2.2}{\percent} of the true
\SI{100}{\micro\second} average over the $448$ pairs above \SI{2}{\electronvolt}
(\texttt{validation/trajectory\_census/}), $45$
of $448$ point-and-direction pairs above \SI{2}{\electronvolt} exceed
\SI{10}{\percent}, the worst being \SI{17.5}{\percent} at
$\Te = \SI{2.02}{\electronvolt}$ and $\nel = \SI{1.93e13}{\per\cubic\centi\metre}$.
At the \SI{506}{\micro\second} extrapolated to the ITER pedestal
\cite[Sec.~5]{Loarte2003} the count falls to $24$ and the worst case to
\SI{15.3}{\percent}, the $24$ being a strict subset of the $45$. Both are
fixed-density counts, and Section~\ref{sec:joint_step} steps the density too:
the count barely moves, at $10$ to \SI{11}{\percent} of the pairs available
whichever way the density goes, while the worst case rises to
\SI{22.1}{\percent} when the density steps up with the temperature. The
fixed-density map understates the worst case rather than overstating it.
At densities where the Balmer inversion is ordinarily applied,
$\nel\ge\SI{e14}{\per\cubic\centi\metre}$, none of $108$ pairs exceed
\SI{10}{\percent} and the worst is \SI{7.2}{\percent}. The conditions to
distrust are the tenuous and cool ones, and the error grows toward low density
and low temperature together.

\paragraph{Timescale separation gives no warning.} A modeller deciding whether
to trust a QSS-based inversion reaches for $\Msep = \tauslow/\taurel$, which is
the ratio that licenses eliminating the fast variables in the first place. It
does not locate the error. Inside the defended range
$\Te\ge\SI{2}{\electronvolt}$ the two maxima sit on the same temperature row
$19.3\times$ apart in density: at the point of largest $\Msep$ the plateau error
is \SI{7.4}{\percent}, the 71st percentile of the $448$ defended pairs and a
factor $2.5$ below the worst
(Section~\ref{sec:M_does_not_predict}). What survives once the operating point
is controlled for is not an absence of information but information with the
wrong sign: residualising both $\log\Msep$ and $\log\epsplat$ on saturated
two-way fixed effects in $\Te$ and $\nel$ leaves a partial correlation of
$-0.617$, so the little that $\Msep$ carries about the error it carries
backwards, and it is a second-order statement about the $\Te$--$\nel$
interaction rather than a ranking. The reason is visible in Eq.~\eqref{eq:recipe}. $\Msep$
compares two rates, and $\epsplat$ measures a distance, the product of a
sensitivity with a displacement, neither of which appears in $\Msep$. A large
$\Msep$ certifies that the excited states have equilibrated with the reservoir.
It says nothing about whether the reservoir is in the right place. Nor is this an
artefact of how well $\Msep$ is known: replacing the excitation rate
coefficients with an independent calculation moves it by \SI{11}{\percent}
while leaving its ordering across the grid intact, rank correlation $0.99993$
(Section~\ref{sec:M_does_not_predict}).

\paragraph{A second failure, and it is the same cancellation at its limit.} The bound of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sbar_bound} constrains the error in the ratio, and a
diagnostician does not suffer an error in the ratio; she inverts it. The
inversion divides by $\partial\ln\Robs/\partial\ln\Te$, and that derivative
passes through zero. Scanning temperature at
$\nel = \SI{5.18e13}{\per\cubic\centi\metre}$, the fractional distance
$\epsstep$ between the two tabulated answers either side of the step falls to
$4.9\times10^{-5}$ near \SI{6.9}{\electronvolt}
(Section~\ref{sec:inversion_jacobian}), which means the tabulated ratio is
stationary in temperature there. The $n=3/n=4$ ratio is locally non-invertible
for temperature at that point, so a bounded error in the observable produces an
unbounded error in the inferred temperature. It afflicts a perfectly
equilibrated plasma, and it is not unrelated to the transient error. The slope
of the tabulated ratio in temperature is the residual of a cancellation between
the manifold's own response $P$ and the reservoir's $SG$, the two
equal and opposite to a median \SI{6.8}{\percent} over the defended scope
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:slope_two_channels}); the fold is where that residual passes
through zero, and the same residual is the denominator of every inversion. For a practitioner
it is the more dangerous of the two failures, and it was found only because
$\epsstep$ was computed and kept rather than discarded. Its locus across the
rest of the grid has not been mapped, and Section~\ref{sec:inversion_jacobian}
records what would close that.

\paragraph{The table misreads the transient by an order of magnitude.} Pushed
through the table at known density, the plateau ratio has no solution within
the table's $1$ to \SI{10}{\electronvolt} domain for $166$ of the $448$
defended pairs, every one a cooling step, and over
the $238$ pairs with a unique fold-free answer a \SI{4.81}{\percent}
temperature step is read back with a median error of \SI{48}{\percent} and a
worst of \SI{144}{\percent}, on the side the plasma came from at every pair
(Section~\ref{sec:inversion_measured}). The amplification, median $8.3$, is the
ratio of the reservoir term to the residual slope of the table, and is large
because the two responses above nearly cancel. At the two highest densities it
falls to
$3.2$ and the median error to \SI{16}{\percent}. These are plateau values at
known density; neither the time averages of Section~\ref{sec:persistence} nor
the two-parameter inversion has been carried through the table.

\paragraph{What does not follow.} None of the above licenses a claim about a
particular machine. Every number in this section is conditional on the
ground-state neutral density being frozen for the duration of the event, which
is the conditional Section~\ref{sec:transport} states and cannot test from
inside a zero-dimensional model.

\section{What is new, at the smallest size that is defensible}
\label{sec:novelty}
% QUESTION: which parts of the argument are this work's, and which were
% established decades ago?
%=============================================================================

Section~\ref{sec:prior_work} set out what was already known before this work
began. What follows is the narrower question of what may now be claimed that
could not be claimed then, item by item, and it is deliberately smaller than the
argument might suggest.

\paragraph{The two-channel decomposition is not new and is not claimed.} That
every excited population is exactly affine in the ground-state and ion
densities, Eq.~\eqref{eq:two_supplies_intro}, is the population-coefficient
decomposition of Bates, Kingston and McWhirter, written in the Saha-normalised
form $b(p) = r_0(p) + r_1(p)\,b(1)$ by Fujimoto and McWhirter
\cite{Bates1962, FujimotoMcWhirter1990, Fujimoto2004}. Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}
says so where it uses it. Everything structural in this thesis is a consequence
of that 1962 identity.

\paragraph{The logistic form is a rewriting, and should be called one.} Writing
the ground-fed fraction in the logarithmic reservoir variable turns
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ground_fed_fraction} into the single logistic of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:f_logistic}, so the two shells' fractions are the same curve
displaced. This is one line of algebra applied to a published formula, and the
same function appears in chemical kinetics as the Michaelis--Menten saturation
fraction, where a substrate concentration plays the part of the reservoir. The
defensible statement is that it has not previously been stated in this form in
the collisional--radiative literature. It is not a new function and it is not a
new result about hydrogen.

\paragraph{The bound is old, it is old in several fields at once, and only the
assembly is claimed.} That $\max|\ffrac{3}-\ffrac{4}| = \tanh(|\Delta|/4) < 1$
follows in a few steps from two displaced logistics. A literature search
conducted for this thesis found each of its parts already in print, in four
separate literatures, the earliest by more than a century.

The \emph{value} is Yule's coefficient of colligation~\cite{Yule1912}. Two
unit-width logistic curves separated by $\Delta$ have constant odds ratio
$\omega = e^{\Delta}$, and Yule's $Y = (\sqrt{\omega}-1)/(\sqrt{\omega}+1)$ is
identically $\tanh(\tfrac14\ln\omega)$. The two agree to machine precision at
every $\Delta$ tested. The same number is the maximum risk difference at fixed
odds ratio, attained at baseline $1/(1+\sqrt{\omega})$.

The \emph{location} of the maximum is Samejima's~\cite{Samejima1969}: the
difference of two shifted logistic ogives is the graded-response operating
characteristic, and she states it is unimodal and symmetric about the midpoint.
She does not give the height.

The \emph{general theorem} is Anderson's~\cite{Anderson1955}: for a symmetric
unimodal density, $\sup_x[F(x)-F(x-\Delta)] = 2F(\Delta/2)-1$. This claim is its
logistic instance, and the object itself is the L\'evy concentration function.

What the search did \emph{not} find is the bound in the
collisional--radiative literature, and one negative is worth recording because
it was the largest remaining publication risk. Fujimoto's
\emph{Plasma Spectroscopy} Chapter~4 \cite{Fujimoto2004} is where the
population coefficients $r_0(p)$ and $r_1(p)$ that $\ffrac{p}$ is built from are
introduced, tabulated and analysed at length. \textbf{It contains no $\tanh$, no
logistic, and no bound on the difference of two population coefficients};
searching the printed chapter for each of those returns nothing. The
normalisation it does establish, $r_0(p) + r_1(p) \to 1$ at high density, is the
statement that the two channels exhaust the population, which is what makes
$\ffrac{p}$ a fraction in the first place and is assumed rather than derived
here. The bound is not implicit in that chapter.

The corollary $|\mathrm{d}\ln\Robs/\mathrm{d}\ln\bdep| < 1$ is likewise not new.
That $\ffrac{p}\in(0,1)$ is the standard statement that substrate elasticities
lie between zero and the Hill coefficient~\cite{CornishBowden2012}, and a
general bound on the logarithmic sensitivity of a \emph{ratio} of steady-state
observables, reducing to unity when a single state carries the
perturbation, is Owen and Horowitz's~\cite{OwenHorowitz2023}. The companion
identity $\int(\ffrac{3}-\ffrac{4})\,\mathrm{d}\ln\bdep = \Delta$ is Raju's
unsigned area for equal-discrimination curves~\cite{Raju1988}.

The qualitative content has an empirical face in this field already. Verhaegh
and co-workers report that a unique solution for the recombining fraction from a
measured Balmer ratio exists only for $F_{\mathrm{rec}}$ between about $0.15$
and $0.85$~\cite{Verhaegh2019}, which is the saturation of $\ffrac{p}$ observed
directly rather than derived.

What is left, and what this thesis claims, is the \emph{assembly}: that these
are one object; that it bounds the diagnostic error of a hydrogen Balmer ratio
with respect to its ground-state reservoir, uniformly over every atomic dataset;
and that the bound is near-tight exactly where the error is largest. The claim
is deliberately written so that finding an older statement of any single part
costs it nothing, because that has now happened four times.

The corollary requires $n_p = A_p + B_p u$ with $A_p$ and $B_p$ independent of
$u$. Where that fails, effective Hill coefficients well above unity are
reachable, and two Michaelian enzymes competing for a shared substrate is the
standard counterexample. It does not arise here, because $u$ is an external
reservoir rather than a pool the levels compete for.

\paragraph{Greenland stated the negative result in general form, twenty-five
years ago.} That the validity criteria for a reduced CR model are conditions on
eigenvectors rather than eigenvalues, and are not related to the equilibrium
timescales, is Greenland's, in two papers of the same year and separately
attributable \cite{Greenland2001a, Greenland2001b}. This work does not improve
on either statement. It quantifies them for one diagnostic and maps where the
criterion misleads: the $19.3\times$ separation of the two maxima inside the
defended range, the sign reversal of the association once the operating point
is controlled for, and the census of
Section~\ref{sec:M_does_not_predict}. Turning a general criterion into
a number is a smaller contribution than stating the criterion, and it is the one
available here.

\paragraph{Sawada and Fujimoto asked the adjacent question and answered it
first.} Their paper is titled, in part, for the validity range of the
quasi-steady-state solution of the coupled rate equations
\cite{SawadaFujimoto1994}, and it treats the same system: atomic hydrogen, a CR
model, an abrupt change of conditions. What they established is which level
controls the approach to steady state. In an ionising plasma the levels below
Griem's boundary relax at their own natural lifetimes while those above relax at
the rate of the boundary level, and in a recombining plasma the roles invert.
Their answer is a clock internal to the excited manifold. The question here is
what a diagnostic inversion costs while the reservoir underneath that manifold
has not settled, which is a different quantity governed by a clock four orders
of magnitude slower, and which can be large at the very moment the excited-level
solution is exact.

The separation between the two papers is structural rather than a matter of
emphasis, and the paper states it itself. Setting out their method, they write
that \enquote{under the ionizing plasma condition $n(1)$ is kept constant and
$n_z$ is set equal to zero, and vice versa under the recombining plasma
condition} \cite[p.~5566]{SawadaFujimoto1994}, having already assumed that the
electron density, the ion density and the electron temperature
\enquote{are given} \cite[p.~5565]{SawadaFujimoto1994}. \textbf{The reservoir is
a fixed parameter in their calculation.} It cannot be stale, because it never
moves, and the question this thesis asks cannot arise inside their framework.

That is not one paper's choice. The same calculation is the worked example of
Appendix~4B of Fujimoto's \emph{Plasma Spectroscopy}, whose figures are
captioned as quoted from that paper, and the appendix sets it up the same way:
neutral atoms are immersed in a plasma at $t=0$ and, in its words, ``if $n(1)$
is kept constant, after the initial transient this system should reduce to the
high-temperature ionizing plasma'' treated earlier in the chapter
\cite[App.~4B]{Fujimoto2004}. \textbf{The frozen reservoir is how the standard
textbook treatment is posed}, which makes the gap this thesis fills a gap in the
tradition rather than an oversight in one paper.

The two-channel decomposition this thesis is built on is their Eq.~(3),
$n(p) = R_0(p)\,n_z n_e + R_1(p)\,n(1)\,n_e$, and the ground-state equation
whose eigenvalue is the slow clock of Chapter~\ref{ch:theory} is their Eq.~(4),
$\mathrm{d}n(1)/\mathrm{d}t = -S_{\mathrm{CR}} n(1) n_e
+ \alpha_{\mathrm{CR}} n_z n_e$. They write Eq.~(4) and, having fixed $n(1)$,
do not solve it. This thesis solves it and reads the diagnostic consequence off
the solution. That is the whole of the gap, and it is now a matter of record
rather than an inference from their emphasis.

Their validity criterion for a temporal change in $\Te$, their Eq.~(23),
$|(\tau_{\mathrm{res}}/T)\,E(1,p_G)/k\Te| \ll 1$ with $T$ the time constant of
the change, bounds whether the excited levels keep up. No companion condition on
the reservoir appears anywhere in the paper, and none is needed there, because
the reservoir is held fixed.

One apparent conflict is worth resolving before an examiner raises it. For a
true step $T \to 0$, so Eq.~(23) is violated, while
Section~\ref{sec:qss_is_exact} reports $\epsQSS = 8.66\times10^{-6}$ for exactly
such a step. The two statements are about different intervals. Equation~(23)
governs the settling itself, and during the first few nanoseconds after a step
the excited-state closure genuinely does fail. $\epsQSS$ is evaluated on the
plateau, after the manifold has settled, where it holds to a part in $10^{5}$ to
$10^{8}$. Read that way the two results are complementary rather than merely
compatible, and they partition the transient between them.

Their worked case, $\nel = \SI{e12}{\per\cubic\centi\metre}$ and
$\Te = \SI{10}{\electronvolt}$, sits on this thesis's own grid, which is what
makes the comparison a comparison rather than an analogy.

\paragraph{What is claimable.} The cost of the ionisation-balance
closure hidden inside a two-parameter Balmer table is now a computable number at
an arbitrary operating point, expressed through two structural coefficients that
are properties of the plasma rather than of the disturbance, together with a
ceiling on it that no atomic dataset can raise and a map of where in
$(\Te,\nel)$ the coefficients are large. That is the contribution, and it is
smaller than the question Chapter~\ref{ch:intro} asked.

\section{What this work cannot claim}
\label{sec:implications}
% QUESTION: what would an examiner be entitled to strike out?
%=============================================================================

These are carried forward from Chapter~\ref{ch:limits} without re-argument, and
they bound every statement in Section~\ref{sec:criterion}.

\paragraph{Everything quantitative is conditional on a frozen reservoir, and the
condition is measured to fail.} If neutral transport and recycling renew $\ngs$
faster than the local atomic physics does, the ground state is not stale and the
error mechanism does not operate. Comparing a charge-exchange-limited neutral
escape time with $\tauslow$ pair by pair, not one of the $45$ breakdown pairs
above \SI{2}{\electronvolt} satisfies $\tauesc > \tauslow$, at either
\SI{10}{\centi\metre} or \SI{20}{\centi\metre} to the boundary, while $27$ to
\SI{36}{\percent} of the other $635$ analysed pairs do
(Section~\ref{sec:transport_selection}). The selection is structural: a pair
enters the census because $\tauslow$ is long, and a long $\tauslow$ is exactly the
condition under which transport rather than ionisation sets $\ngs$. The
magnitudes of Chapter~\ref{ch:results} are therefore upper estimates conditional
on an assumption this model cannot satisfy at the points that carry them. The
structural results of Section~\ref{sec:criterion}, being properties of
$\Lmat(\Te,\nel)$ at fixed conditions, are untouched by it
(Section~\ref{sec:transport_partition}).

\paragraph{It is not a divertor result below two electron-volts, and the
restriction is not a hedge.} Holding $\nel$ fixed while the ground-state
population changes is self-consistent only where the plasma is largely ionised.
Above \SI{2}{\electronvolt} conservation of nuclei across one temperature step
demands $|\delta\nel/\nel| < 5.4\times10^{-3}$. Below it, $68$ of $392$ one-step
operators require $|\delta\nel/\nel| > \SI{10}{\percent}$ and $36$ require more
than \SI{100}{\percent} (Section~\ref{sec:quasineutrality}).
$\Te\ge\SI{2}{\electronvolt}$ is the only self-consistent region of the grid,
not a cautious subset of it, and the cold-edge numbers are asymptotic values of
a model outside its own domain.

\paragraph{It is not detachment.} The density crest sits a factor $5.2$ below
the detached band, and at citable detached densities the \SI{100}{\micro\second} time-averaged estimate
never reaches \SI{10}{\percent} (Section~\ref{sec:not_detachment}). The crest is
a property of the atomic system and needs no divertor geometry to produce it.

\paragraph{There are no molecular channels, in a gas that is largely
molecular.} A third supply feeding $n=3$ preferentially would change
$\ffrac{3}-\ffrac{4}$. Imposing molecular emissivity fractions of $0.60$ at
$n=3$ and $0.30$ at $n=4$ on the model's own operator reduces the cold-corner
plateau error by a factor of $6.1$, and the upper end of the reported ranges,
$(0.70,0.45)$, by a factor of $9.4$
(\texttt{validation/molecular\_channel/}). Those figures apply to the cold
corner, which lies outside the defended range in any case; at the crest the
same two targets give $5.4$ and $8.1$. The reduction is larger than a dilution
linear in $\ffrac{3}-\ffrac{4}$ suggests, because $\epsplat$ is exponential in
the sensitivity, and it is not monotone: the diluted difference passes through
zero near $\phi_3 = 0.74$, past which the error grows again with the
sensitivity reversed.
The estimate is built in Section~\ref{sec:molecules} from the emissivity
fractions cited there, driven through this thesis's atomic operator rather than
computed from a matrix containing molecules, and it
inherits every uncertainty in those fractions. An earlier draft of this chapter
recorded the two chapters as contradicting each other on whether a bound could
be constructed at all; Section~\ref{sec:molecules} constructs one, and this
paragraph quotes it with the same caveats.

\paragraph{The atomic-data systematic rests on one substitution, not on a
survey of rate sets.} Two of the three input
channels have been swapped for independent calculations and carried through to
the observable: the excitation coefficients for R-matrix values, and the
ionisation coefficients --- and with them the three-body recombination that
detailed balance builds from them --- for Lotz. Together they move $\epsplat$
by \SI{15.5}{\percent} over the $448$ defended pairs and at most
\SI{25.4}{\percent}, and $\Msep$ by \SI{11}{\percent}
(Sections~\ref{sec:atomic_propagation} and~\ref{sec:bound}). Those figures are
the difference between two calculations, not an uncertainty: they say how far
the answer moves between the datasets that happen to exist, and a third dataset
could lie outside the interval the two define. Three gaps remain in the bound.
Excitation transitions reaching $n\ge6$ have no independent counterpart.
Ionisation above $n=9$ is Lotz in both the model and the substitution, so those
shells are compared with nothing. And radiative recombination has one source
only, tested at its own quoted accuracy rather than against a rival, which is a
weaker statement even though the \SI{1.1}{\percent} it produces is small.

\paragraph{The density maximum is a range, not a density.} It lies between
$7\times10^{12}$ and \SI{5e13}{\per\cubic\centi\metre}, resolved to about a
factor of $3$, because every density row sits above \SI{90}{\percent} of its own
maximum across roughly a decade (Section~\ref{sec:results_summary}). Quoting a single density for it overstates what
the grid resolves. That factor of $3$ is a whole-grid figure and its upper end
is set by the three coldest rows, the only ones whose maximum falls on
\SI{5.18e13}{\per\cubic\centi\metre}; inside the defended range every row
maximises on \SI{1.93e13}{\per\cubic\centi\metre} and the subgrid vertex spans
$1.45$ to $\SI{1.95e13}{\per\cubic\centi\metre}$, a factor $1.35$
(\texttt{validation/crest\_subgrid/}). The range is wide because of the rows
this thesis excludes, not because the crest is poorly located where it is
defended. It is also the $(3,4)$ shell pair's maximum and not the
Balmer series' (Section~\ref{sec:ridge_conditional}).

\section{What would settle what is open}
\label{sec:further_work}
% QUESTION: which unfinished calculations would change what may be claimed?
%=============================================================================

Four calculations would change the standing of a result in this thesis. The
first two are bounded work on the model as it stands; the other two are
different projects.

\paragraph{A density grid fine enough to name an ELM.} The joint map of
Section~\ref{sec:joint_step} crosses temperature steps with density steps of one
grid index, and one density index is a factor $2.683$, that is
$+\SI{168}{\percent}$, against $+\SI{4.81}{\percent}$ for one temperature index.
The reservoir is roughly fifty times less sensitive to density than to
temperature in logarithmic terms, so the two contributions end up comparable in
that sweep only because the density grid is that coarse. The coarseness belongs
to the tabulation and not to ELMs, which is why
Table~\ref{tab:joint_census} is quoted per density step rather than as one
number. Regenerating $\Lmat$ on a density grid spaced like the temperature grid
would let the joint census be quoted at a realistic ELM density excursion, and
it is the one remaining calculation that would move a number this thesis
reports. It costs the same as the map that already exists.

\paragraph{An ionisation dataset that reaches the top of the ladder.} The
substitutions of Sections~\ref{sec:atomic_propagation} and~\ref{sec:bound}
bound $\epsplat$ against independent data wherever independent data exist,
which is $n\le5$ for excitation and $n\le9$ for ionisation. Above $n=9$ the
model and the substitution both use Lotz, so the six shells whose feed is
essentially all three-body are the shells with no independent check at all ---
and they are the shells where Section~\ref{sec:atomic_uncertainty} records Lotz
exceeding CCC by a factor of four to eight, and where
Chapter~\ref{ch:limits} finds the terminal shell over-populated by
4.9--6.2 times. Extending a close-coupling ionisation calculation to
$n=10$--$15$, or bounding those coefficients some other way, would both close
the last gap in the atomic-data bound and test whether those two observations
are the same one. As it stands the link between them remains a hypothesis.

\paragraph{A two-region or transport-coupled calculation.} The single conditional
on which all of Section~\ref{sec:criterion} rests is that $\ngs$ is frozen for
the duration of the event. Deciding it requires $\ngs$ to be supplied by a
recycling influx from an edge transport calculation rather than solved for from
$\Lmat\nvec + \Svec\nion = 0$, which is a different class of calculation and
not a refinement of this one. It is the item that would convert the result from
a statement about a class of CR model into a statement about a machine.

\paragraph{Molecular channels, which may change the form and not just the
coefficients.}
Adding molecules as states inside $\Lmat$, fed from the same two reservoirs,
leaves the structural results untouched, and Section~\ref{sec:molecules} says
so. Adding molecular hydrogen as a \emph{third independent reservoir} does not.
The population balance then reads
$n_p = a_p\ngs + c_p\nion + m_p n_{\mathrm{H_2}}$, with $n_{\mathrm{H_2}}$ the
molecular density in \si{\per\cubic\centi\metre} and $m_p$ the dimensionless
number of atoms in level $p$ supported per molecule. The observable then
depends on two independent reservoir ratios rather than one, and the ground-fed fraction is no longer a single logistic in a single
variable. The bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sbar_bound} is a statement about a
one-dimensional family of curves and there is no reason to expect it to survive
in that form. Which of the two descriptions a real detaching divertor requires
is a physics question this work does not answer.

\medskip

The physicist at the console keeps her spectrometer, her ratio and her table.
What she gains is a test she can apply before she trusts the answer: evaluate
$\overline{S}$ and $G$ at the operating point she is looking at, multiply by the
fractional temperature excursion her plasma has just undergone, and compare the
result with the precision she needs. If the plasma has been quiet for several
times $\tauslow$, the table is telling her about the plasma. If it has not, the
table is telling her about a plasma that no longer exists, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:recipe} says by how much.
